>"For the modern physicists, then, Shiva's dance is the dance of subatomic matter. As in Hindu mythology, it is a continual dance of creation and destruction involving the whole cosmos; the basis of all existence and of all natural phenomena." - Fritjof Capra, author of 'The Tao of Physics'. This quote is inscribed on the plaque of a statue of Shiva in the CERN complex in Geneva, Switzerland.

<span class="dropcap">A</span>ncient Indian writings about cosmology show remarkable depth and insight, and they have provided inspiration for countless physicists around the world. Several kinds of hypotheses have been described regarding the creation of the universe in texts such as the Upanishads and the Puranas. The concept of creation and destruction of the universe and the existence of multiple universes is discussed widely in these texts. 
Perhaps the most interesting philosophy was given by the Nasadiya Sukta from Mandala 10 of the Rugveda. Great astronomer Carl Sagan quoted it while discussing India's “tradition of skeptical questioning and unselfconscious humility before the great cosmic mysteries.” 
The Sukta presents paradoxical statements, such as “There was neither non-existence nor existence then” while describing the beginning of the universe. It explores the possibilities of what could have been before the universe, and concludes with “The Supreme Brahman of the world, all pervasive and all knowing, He indeed knows, if not, no one knows.” 
Here, the ‘supreme brahman (ब्रह्मन्)’ refers to the ultimate reality, the life force, the base of the universe. The Upanishads give several ‘mahavakyas’ or statements such as ‘अहं ब्रह्म अस्मि’ (I am Brahman), सर्वं खल्विदं ब्रह्म (All this is Brahman), प्राणो ब्राह्मणः (Life is Brahman). 
From a modern understanding of physics, everything around us including ourselves is made of matter that was created during the beginning of the universe, which means, in a way we rose from the universe itself and will someday return to it (this idea is discussed in the Chandogya Upanishad). This is the idea of Brahman. 
The Aitreya Upanishad expands on the idea of Brahman in a more poetic manner. It assumes that the Self, or Atman existed prior to the creation of the universe, and it is the Atman which created the universe. Thereafter came man, who with a strong sense of self, asked the fundamental question: कोऽहमिति? Who am I? 
To answer this question the Upanishad says, 

### “Consciousness is what defines man, the source of all intellectual and moral theories, all gods, all living beings (man, animals, plants), all that there is. Then the Upanishad asserts that the key to the riddle of the Universe is one's own inner self. To know the universe, know thyself. Become immortal, suggests the Aitareya Upanishad, by being you.” *- translated by Paul Deussen*
and
### “Everything is led (produced) by knowledge. It rests on Knowledge. The world is led by Knowledge. Knowledge is its cause. Knowledge is Brahman.” *- translated by Max Muller.*

This perspective on metaphysics (the study of reality) and cosmology presented by the Upanishads is a very fascinating and interesting philosophy which bears resemblance to modern scientific concepts.
